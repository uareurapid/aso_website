App Store Localization for Indie Developers: Easy Wins in Non-English Markets

Published: June 26, 2026 Topic: ASO Localization Strategy Read time: 5 minutes

You're leaving downloads on the table. Not because your app is bad, but because 60% of App Store users don't speak English as their first language — and your listing only speaks one.

I see this constantly with indie apps. A beautifully built utility or game with zero localization. The developer assumes "everyone speaks English anyway." They don't. And Apple knows it.

Let's talk about how to fix this without hiring translators or rebuilding your app.

Why Localization Hits Harder Than You Think

Apple's search algorithm favors localized listings. When someone in Mexico searches for "editor de fotos," the App Store doesn't just translate your English keywords — it checks if you actually have a Spanish listing.

If you don't, you're invisible.

Here's what happens with a localized listing:

  • Keyword index multiplies. Each locale gets its own 100-character keyword field. Localize into 5 languages and you get 500 characters of keyword real estate.
  • Conversion rate climbs. Users convert at 2-3x higher rates when the listing is in their native language. People trust what they can read.
  • Competition drops. Most indie devs skip localization. The markets you enter are far less crowded than English.

I analyzed a productivity app on ASO Analytics that localized into Spanish, French, and Portuguese. Their downloads jumped 47% in 30 days — with zero changes to the app itself. Just the listing.

The 80/20 of ASO Localization

You don't need to localize everything. Here's the order of impact:

1. Keywords (Highest Impact, Lowest Effort)

This is the cheat code. Apple gives each locale its own keyword field. You can stuff region-specific keywords without affecting your English keywords.

Example: A habit tracker app targeting Spain might use:

`` hábitos, rutina diaria, objetivos, seguimiento, productividad personal, organizador, recordatorios, planificador día, tasks ``

These are keywords that Spanish-speaking users actually type — not translations of your English keywords. "Recordatorios" (reminders) is a completely different volume play than "reminders" in English.

Actionable step: Use ASO Analytics to look up what keywords your competitors rank for in target countries. Don't guess — steal the data.

2. App Title & Subtitle

This is the second-biggest lever. Apple weights the title heavily in search, and a localized title + subtitle can double your keyword coverage.

Don't just translate word-for-word. Adapt for search intent.

| English | Direct Translation | Search-Optimized | |---------|-------------------|------------------| | "Habit Tracker - Daily Goals" | "Rastreador de Hábitos - Metas Diarias" | "Habit Tracker - Seguimiento de Hábitos y Metas" | | "Photo Editor Pro" | "Editor de Fotos Pro" | "Editor de Fotos - Retoca y Mejora tus Imágenes" |

Notice the search-optimized versions keep the brand name but pack the subtitle with high-volume local keywords.

Actionable step: For each target language, research the top 3 keywords by volume. Weave them naturally into subtitle.

3. Description (First 3 Lines)

The full description carries less SEO weight than it used to, but the first 2-3 lines appear above the fold before "Read More." That preview text matters for conversion — especially in localized markets where users scan fast.

Don't translate your entire description if you're bootstrapped. Start with the opening paragraph and feature bullets. That's where eyes land.

4. Screenshots (Last, But Don't Skip)

If you're serious about a market, localize your screenshot text. A French user seeing English screenshots on a French listing feels like a half-finished product.

Tools like Figma with component variants make this manageable. Build once, swap text layers per locale.

Which Languages Should You Localize First?

Don't do all 40 languages. Start with 3-5 that actually move the needle.

Here's a priority framework based on App Store data:

Tier 1: Spanish, French, Portuguese

  • Spanish: 500M+ speakers worldwide, massive App Store presence in Spain, Mexico, and across Latin America. Mexico alone is a top-10 App Store market.
  • French: France, Canada, Belgium, Switzerland, and large portions of Africa. High ARPU (average revenue per user) in France.
  • Portuguese: Brazil is the 5th largest App Store market by downloads. Portuguese localization opens all of it — and Portugal.

Tier 2: German, Japanese, Korean

  • German: Germany, Austria, Switzerland. High spending per user.
  • Japanese: Japan is the #3 App Store market by revenue. Users strongly prefer native-language apps. But localization quality matters more here — poor Japanese hurts more than no Japanese.
  • Korean: South Korea is a top-5 gaming market with high conversion expectations.

Tier 3: Simplified Chinese, Arabic, Russian

  • Larger effort, larger reward. Only tackle these once Tier 1-2 are solid.

Actionable step: Pull your App Store Connect analytics. Sort countries by impressions. Pick the top 3 non-English countries and localize for those languages.

Translation Quality: Good Enough vs. Perfect

Here's the honest truth: "Good enough" localization beats no localization every time.

A few years ago, "good enough" meant embarrassing Google Translate output. That's changed. GPT-4 and Claude produce translations that are 90-95% native quality — plenty for an App Store listing.

The workflow I recommend:

  1. Generate translations with an LLM. Feed it your English listing + context about your app's tone.
  2. Run it past a native speaker. Pay $20-50 on Upwork for a 15-minute review. They'll catch idioms the AI missed.
  3. Ship it. Don't obsess over perfection. You can iterate.

I've seen developers wait months trying to find "the perfect translator" while losing hundreds of downloads daily. Ship the 90% version now. Polish later.

One Thing Apple Doesn't Tell You

Localized keywords stack.

If your app name contains "Photo" and someone searches "foto" in Spain, Apple doesn't match them unless your Spanish listing uses "foto" in its keywords. Same word, different language, zero cross-matching.

This means localizing literally multiplies your keyword surface area. An English-only listing might rank for 20 keywords meaningfully. A listing localized into 4 languages can rank for 80+.

Common Localization Pitfalls

Don't just copy-paste English keywords into other locales. "Card game" doesn't translate to high-volume "juego de cartas" directly — the keyword volumes, competition, and search patterns are entirely different per market. Research each locale independently.

Don't forget cultural context. A meditation app calling itself "Calm" works in English. In German-speaking markets, users search for "Meditation" or "Achtsamkeit" (mindfulness). Fit the local search behavior, not just the translation.

Don't localize and forget. Keyword trends shift per market. What's hot in Brazil this month may cool next month. Track rankings per locale.

The Bottom Line

Localization is the highest-ROI ASO move most indie devs ignore. It's not glamorous. It won't get you a Product Hunt feature. But it will quietly add 30-50% more downloads to your app with a few hours of work.

Start with Spanish. Add French and Portuguese next. Use keyword data, not guesses. Ship the 90% version today.

Then track what happens — you might be surprised.
