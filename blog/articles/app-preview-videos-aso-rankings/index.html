App Preview Videos: Do They Actually Help Rankings?

I get this question a lot from indie devs: "Should I spend a weekend making an App Preview video? Will it help my rankings?"

Short answer: App Preview videos don't directly boost your keyword rankings. They do something better — they make the rankings you already have actually produce downloads.

Long answer: it's more nuanced than that. Let's dig in.

What App Preview Videos Actually Do

Apple introduced App Preview videos back in 2014, and they've evolved significantly since then. Here's what matters for ASO in 2026:

Auto-play in search results. When your app appears in the top 1-2 positions for a search, the preview video auto-plays silently. No tap required. The user sees a looping 30-second clip of your app in action. That's free real estate your competitors without videos aren't getting.

They replace your first screenshot in search. This is the critical detail most devs miss. If you add a preview video, it replaces screenshot #1 in the search results carousel. That means your video IS your first impression. Make it count.

They boost conversion, not rankings. Apple has never confirmed preview videos as a direct ranking factor. But conversion rate — the percentage of viewers who tap through and install — is absolutely a signal the algorithm tracks. If your video doubles your search-to-install conversion, your app becomes more valuable for the keywords you're ranking on. Apple wants to surface apps people actually download.

Here's the chain: Better preview → higher tap-through rate → more installs → stronger conversion signal → potential ranking improvement. It's indirect, but it's real.

The Numbers: What Happens When You Add One

I've tracked this on ASO Analytics across multiple indie apps, and the pattern is consistent:

  • 15-25% lift in search result tap-through rate — just from adding a competent preview video
  • Strongest impact on competitive keywords — where users have 5+ apps to choose from, the video helps you stand out
  • Near-zero impact on branded searches — if someone's searching your app name, they're already sold

A meditation app I tracked added a preview video showing 5 seconds of morning routine UI, 5 seconds of sleep tracking, and 20 seconds of meditation session flow. Their tap-through rate on the keyword "meditation" jumped 22% in the first week. No other changes.

But here's the flip side: I've also seen developers upload previews that actively hurt conversion. More on that in a minute.

What Makes a Preview Video Work

Apple gives you up to 3 preview videos (30 seconds each), auto-playing silently. After 12 years of App Store data, the winning formula is surprisingly consistent:

1. Show the UI in the first 3 seconds

No logos. No title cards. No "Welcome to MyApp!" animations. Users are scrolling fast and auto-play starts immediately. If the first 3 seconds is a fade-in logo, you've lost them.

Start with your app's main screen. Show someone using it. The goal is instant recognition: "Oh, this is a habit tracker. I see the interface. That looks clean."

2. Design for silent viewing

Most App Preview views happen without sound. Don't rely on voiceover or music to tell your story. Use text overlays sparingly — one or two words max, like "Daily Tracking" or "Share with Friends."

Captions are fine. Narration is wasted bytes.

3. Show actual functionality, not animation

The worst preview videos are motion-graphics montages with bouncing icons and "REVOLUTIONARY!" text flying in from the sides. The best ones are screen recordings of real app usage, sped up slightly, showing real workflows.

Users want to see what your app looks like when they're using it. Give them that.

4. One flow per video, three videos max

If your app has multiple core features, make separate videos for each. Apple lets you upload up to three, and they'll auto-play the one that matches the user's device type (iPhone vs iPad).

A task manager might have:

  • Video 1: Quick capture and organize (15 sec)
  • Video 2: Calendar view and scheduling (15 sec)
  • Video 3: Collaboration and sharing (15 sec)

Each one is a focused demo of a single workflow. Together they tell the full story.

5. Portrait orientation, device-correct resolution

1920×886 for iPhone, 1920×1080 for iPad. Apple rejects videos that don't match. Frame your content so it looks native — no letterboxing, no visible status bars from screen recording software.

Use Apple's own guidelines for specs. They change occasionally, and getting rejected wastes days.

When Preview Videos Backfire

Not every app should have one. Here's when skipping the preview is actually the better move:

Your screenshots are already converting at 15%+. If your screenshot game is dialed in and conversion is strong, a mediocre video can dilute that. Don't fix what isn't broken.

Your app's UI isn't polished. The App Preview video shows your actual interface, at full resolution, auto-playing. If your design looks amateur or inconsistent, you're better off with carefully designed screenshot mockups that present the best version of your app.

You can't commit to updating it. A preview video showing features from two versions ago signals neglect. If your app updates frequently and you can't keep the video current, stick with screenshots that are easier to swap.

Your app is a utility with no visual flow. A calculator app doesn't benefit much from a video of someone typing numbers. A VPN app's preview would just be... a toggle switch. Know when visuals add nothing.

Real Examples: Who's Getting It Right

Open the App Store and search "habit tracker." Notice which apps have preview videos in the top results. The ones that auto-play generally show:

  1. The home screen immediately — today's habits with checkmarks
  2. A quick interaction — tapping a habit to mark it complete
  3. Stats or streaks view — the dopamine hit of progress
  4. End on a compelling visual — a calendar with all green checkmarks

The entire thing is 20-25 seconds and tells you exactly what the experience feels like.

Now search "photo editor" and compare. The top apps with previews show someone selecting a photo, applying a filter or adjustment, and seeing the before/after. Clean, fast, obvious. No branding, no fluff.

The common thread: show, don't tell. These videos are mini product demos, not commercials.

Should You Make One?

Here's my practical recommendation matrix:

| Your Situation | Recommendation | |---|---| | New app, polished UI, competitive category | ✅ Yes. Make it a priority. The conversion lift on competitive keywords is worth the effort. | | Established app, solid conversion, just maintaining | 🤷 Test it. Run a preview video for 2 weeks, check conversion rates, decide with data. | | Utility app with minimal UI | ❌ Skip it. Invest in better screenshots instead. | | MVP with rough edges | ❌ Skip it. Polish the UI first, then revisit. | | Game | ✅ Yes. Gameplay footage converts dramatically better than static screenshots. |

How to Track Whether It's Working

This is where ASO Analytics comes in. When you add a preview video, watch these metrics:

Impression-to-page-view ratio. Are more people tapping through from search results? This is your primary indicator. If it doesn't move within 2 weeks, your video isn't resonating.

Conversion rate by keyword. Some keywords will see a bigger lift than others. Track them individually to understand where the video adds the most value.

Competitor comparison. Are your competitors using previews? If all the top-ranked apps for your target keyword have videos and you don't, you're at a visual disadvantage in every search result.

The Bottom Line

App Preview videos are a conversion tool, not a ranking lever. But conversion feeds ranking — so the distinction matters less than you'd think.

A well-made preview video that shows your app's best features in the first 3 seconds will almost certainly improve your keyword performance. A rushed one with logos, title cards, and vague animations will probably hurt it.

The bar isn't as high as you think. You don't need a production studio. A clean screen recording, trimmed to 25-30 seconds, showing one clear workflow, starting directly in your UI — that's enough to beat 80% of the previews currently in the App Store.

Most indie apps don't have one. That's your advantage.
