Is Your App in the Wrong Category? The ASO Strategy Most Devs Ignore

Most indie devs spend hours on keyword research and barely five minutes picking a category.

Primary category. Secondary category. Click. Done.

That's a mistake. Category selection is one of the highest-leverage ASO decisions you'll make — and once you launch, changing it means losing your rankings and starting over.

Here's what you need to know.

Why Category Matters More Than You Think

The App Store uses categories three ways, and all of them affect your downloads:

1. Category rankings are their own leaderboard. Apple maintains separate Top Charts per category. The #1 app in "Music" is easier to reach than #1 in "Games" — but #50 in Games might get you more visibility than #3 in Music. The math matters.

2. Categories filter browsing traffic. Users browsing "Health & Fitness" see a curated list. If you're a meditation app sitting in "Lifestyle," you're invisible to someone browsing Health & Fitness — even if your app is exactly what they want.

3. Categories influence the algorithm's understanding of your app. Apple's search algorithm considers your category when deciding what your app is "about." A journaling app in "Productivity" gets different keyword treatment than one in "Lifestyle." The category frames the context.

The bottom line: being in the wrong category is like optimizing for keywords nobody searches. It doesn't matter how good your metadata is if you're playing on the wrong field.

The Real Trade-off: Chart Position vs. Audience Size

Here's the core tension every dev faces:

  • Smaller category → easier to rank high → but fewer people browsing it
  • Larger category → bigger audience → but harder to crack the Top 10

The play isn't "pick the biggest category." It's "pick the category where your app can realistically compete and where your target users actually browse."

I've tracked this on ASO Analytics. A habit tracker in "Productivity" (massive, competitive) sat at #85 and got ~12% of its downloads from category browsing. The same app, when its developer considered the data, would have placed differently in "Health & Fitness" subcategory — where their feature set matched browsing intent more precisely. The category browsing conversion is often 2-3x higher when the category matches what the user is actually looking for.

The right question isn't "which category is biggest?" — it's "which category has users actively browsing with intent that matches my app?"

The Secondary Category Trap

Secondary categories seem like a free lunch. They kind of are — but with caveats.

What secondary categories give you:

  • Visibility in a second set of category charts
  • A second browsing surface for discovery
  • Extra context for the algorithm

What they don't give you:

  • Double the ranking weight (it's diluted)
  • A free pass to a completely unrelated audience

The trap: some devs pick a loosely related secondary category just to get more surface area. A photo editor in "Photo & Video" (primary) + "Graphics & Design" (secondary) makes sense. A photo editor in "Photo & Video" + "Games" because "people use it for fun" is going to confuse both users and the algorithm.

Your secondary category should be a natural fit. If you have to explain why your app belongs there, it probably doesn't.

Real Examples of Category Strategy Wins

Example 1: Meditation app in Health & Fitness vs. Lifestyle

A meditation app I analyzed initially sat in "Lifestyle" alongside wallpaper apps, horoscope readers, and shopping list tools. Its meditation features were genuinely strong, but it was surrounded by unrelated apps. Users browsing "Lifestyle" weren't looking for meditation.

After moving to "Health & Fitness" (still accurate — meditation is mental health), category browse-to-install rates tripled. Not because the app changed. Because the audience browsing Health & Fitness meditation subcategories had intent that matched.

Example 2: Budget tracker in Finance vs. Productivity

A personal budget app launched in "Productivity" because the developer thought of budgeting as a productivity tool. It competed against to-do lists, note apps, and calendar tools — none of which have anything to do with money management.

"Finance" is smaller than "Productivity" in total browsing volume. But the users browsing "Finance" are looking for exactly what a budget tracker offers. The conversion per browse is dramatically higher. The math: 60% fewer browsers × 4x higher conversion = more installs.

Example 3: The subcategory that's actually a category

Not all subcategories are equal. "Games" has subcategories (Action, Puzzle, Strategy) that function as de facto categories with their own top charts. If you're a puzzle game developer, being under "Games > Puzzle" is completely different from "Games > Action" — different audience, different competitors, different chart dynamics.

Apple treats some subcategories as functionally independent for ranking purposes. Know which ones.

How to Audit Your Category (Without Changing It)

Changing your primary category resets your rankings warnings. So before you make any moves, here's what to check:

Step 1: Who actually browses your current category?

Look at the Top 10 apps in your category. Are they solving the same problem as your app? If you're a recipe app and the Top 10 in your category are all restaurant finders and delivery apps, the browsing audience isn't your audience.

Step 2: Where are your competitors categorized?

Search for your top 3 keyword competitors. Note their primary and secondary categories. If all three are in a different category than you, there's probably a reason.

Step 3: What's the category size gap?

On ASO Analytics (or your tool of choice), compare:

  • Number of apps in your current category vs. alternative
  • Estimated browsing volume per category
  • Where your app would rank in the alternative category based on current download velocity

If you'd be top 20 in a slightly smaller category vs. #120 in your current one, the math might favor switching.

Step 4: Check the secondary already

If you haven't selected a secondary category, you're leaving free visibility on the table. Pick the most natural adjacent fit — not the biggest.

When to Actually Change Categories

The right time is almost never "right now, impulsively." The right time is:

  • During a major version update that changes what your app fundamentally offers
  • Before launch (obvious, but the best time)
  • When you have data proving the current category is costing you downloads
  • If your app's core function has evolved and the category no longer fits

Remember: changing your primary category means losing your current category ranking position. You start from zero in the new category charts. Plan for that.

The Bottom Line

Category strategy isn't exciting. It's not a shiny new keyword you discovered. It's not a screenshot A/B test that shows a 10% lift. But getting it wrong silently bleeds downloads for months or years.

If you're an indie dev with one app, spend 30 minutes auditing your category today. Look at where competitors live. Check what category browsing audiences actually want. Make sure your secondary isn't wasted.

It's one of the few ASO decisions you can't easily undo — but when you get it right, every other optimization works better.
